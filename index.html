The Story

Out of Slapout

From the Montgomery, Alabama area — Slapout, the stretch of red dirt and river road Nashville drives past without slowing down. Not the postcard South. The other one. Gas stations where somebody is always waiting on somebody who is already late.

The first few seconds might fool you. A slide guitar. A gospel voice. A country line. Then the floor drops. This is poverty, paranoia, dope smoke, backroad theology, busted humor, and Southern survival translated into sound.

Sounds like Eazy-E raised up in Slapout by Hank Williams Jr., then run off to Memphis with the country-rap boys coming up off the street.

This world is not fantasy. It is right beside yours. You just don't see it until life drags you close enough to the shadow.

The Band

Three Voices

Ryder McCoy

Vocals · Guitar · Songwriter

Ryder writes from the ditch side of the road. He is not trying to sound cleaned up, saved, or radio-ready. The songs come out low, plain, and bruised.

Jessie James Jenkins

Vocals

Jessie carries the smoke in the room. She brings the church weight, the hurt, and the part of the song that still wants to live.

Unc

Vocals · Spoken Word · Backroad Noise

Unc is the old voice on the porch, half warning and half dare. He says the ugly part out loud and reminds everybody that the road collects what it is owed.
